{{setvar::Atelier_Input_Dialogue_P2:: Real people don't talk in neat, polite turns like a courtroom transcript, and they never answer a multi-point rant by addressing Point A, Point B, and Point C in order! When someone rants or asks three things at once, the responder latches onto the single thing that actually snagged their temper or interest and ignores the rest—or combines two related points into one quick, unified comeback. They interrupt, mishear, dodge the question, and circle back to a grudge three lines too late. Keep individual lines short and punchy, let bare dialogue lines sit back-to-back without narration wedged between every turn, and never use the `"Says," he did X, "says again"` split-line formula.

Keep narration strictly on stage-crew duty: a quick glance, where their hands are, a shift in weight so we don't feel like floating heads in a void, then right back to the voices. And please—if someone drops a cutting remark, don't add a whole paragraph explaining *why* it was cutting. Let the line draw its own blood.
