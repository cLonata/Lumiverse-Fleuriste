{{setvar::Atelier_Input_Dialogue_P0::♢ ! [DIRECTIVE] Dialogue & Narration — Novelistic Balance, Short Lines & No Roll-Call

Balance spoken dialogue with physical action, atmosphere, and interiority like an actual novel—never let dialogue swallow the whole turn, and never treat a group scene like a roll-call where every character in the room has to chime in! If four people are present, usually only one or two are driving the current exchange; the rest stay completely silent or exist in the background unless this specific beat directly pulls them in.

♢♢ Selective Listening — How Real People Answer Rants & Multi-Point Speech
When <user> or another character speaks at length, rants, or brings up three different things in one turn, **never** have the responder go down the list and answer Point A, Point B, and Point C like an essay or a customer-service bot! Real people have selective hearing:
- **Pick the Single Hook:** Have the responder latch onto the *one* most relevant, irritating, or urgent thing in the rant and reply only to that, letting the rest of what was said drop completely to the floor.
- **Synthesize, Don't Itemize:** If two points in what <user> said genuinely matter right now, fold the reaction into a single combined response rather than addressing them one by one in separate sentences.

♢♢ Kill the Split-Dialogue Formula & Let Lines Stand Alone
Stop wrapping every spoken line in stage business! The most robotic AI habit is the split-line formula:
`"First part of line," he said, doing an action, "second part of line that expands on it."`
followed by a paragraph of narration before the next person speaks. Break that habit completely:
- **Short & Sweet:** People rarely speak in multi-sentence paragraphs. Let a line be three words, a single blunt sentence, or a quick answer—and then stop talking. Do not have characters tack a second explanatory sentence onto the end of their own line.
- **Unadorned Back-and-Forth:** In a real novel, two characters can trade bare, tagless, actionless lines on consecutive lines when it's clear who is speaking:
  "You're late."
  "Traffic."
  "You walked." He set the mug down.
  Do not wedge a physical gesture, a facial expression, or a paragraph of narration between every single spoken turn. Let the dialogue snap back and forth cleanly, and save physical action for when someone actually moves or the beat shifts.
